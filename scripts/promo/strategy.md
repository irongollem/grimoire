# What works on Instagram

Newest first. Edited by hand; `npm run promo:strategy` and the Socials tab show the latest entries. Numbers are read at the 24 hour mark (`promo:stats`).

## 18 Nov 2026: every new Reel opens on two seconds of bait

Decisions:

- The maintainer: "for future reel type content lets start with 2 seconds of baiting (hate it but thats how these things go live)". Every feature Reel opened on a 1.2 s brand card, the moment Instagram's test audience decides to stay or swipe. A Reel now opens on a hook (`Episode.hook`): two seconds of its own payoff with a bait line, and the brand moves to the close. Posted Reels keep their cut.
- YouTube Shorts go up in the same hourly run as the Instagram post, newest first. `handouts-player` (17 Nov) went to Instagram alone because its title had not been approved; a title is now approved with the caption, before scheduling.

To read: a hooked Reel's watch time and reach against the unhooked feature Reels (the band of 76 to 165 reach).

## 15 Nov 2026: cup-town lost its viewers before the town came up

Findings:

- `cup-town` reached 36 accounts at 24 hours, under every earlier post (the lowest was 76), and was watched 4.4 s of 22.7 (19%). inn-dawn, the same format with the same hashtags at the same hour, was watched 19.6 s (89%).
- The cut held still on a picnic basket for 3 s with no words, the teacup town crested the hill at about 6 s, and its title came at 11.5 s. Most viewers had gone before any of it. Instagram shows a new Reel to a small test audience first and stops there when they swipe away.

Decisions:

- A book nook tells a story (the maintainer: storytelling is the core of D&D). From its first second it carries lines in the DM's read-aloud voice, each matched to what the camera shows, ending on something the picture then answers. Scenes take `story` lines for this.
- The camera moves within the first 2 to 3 s, and the scene's subject is on screen by about 5 s.
- `prison-cell` was recut the same way before it posted: "Night in the cells. The torch outside has burned low." / "The jailer's chair is empty. But you are not alone." Then the dog with the keys.

Experiment started:

- `cup-town-told`: the same painting and song with three story lines and the tilt starting at 2.2 s. A clean test of the opening, since nothing else changed. Compare its 24 h views and watch time with `cup-town`.

## 15 Nov 2026: a nook and a feature on the same tune, a day apart

Experiment started:

- `circus` (19 Nov, a living painting: Brilianto! juggling to his song, "Step right up!") and `songs-short` (20 Nov, the songs page, opening on the same song) go out a day apart. The question: does a feature Reel hold people longer when the day before has already given them its tune? Compare songs-short's watch time with the features' and with circus's.
- `songs-short` replaces the long `songs` Reel on Instagram; posting both would tell the same story twice.

## 14 Nov 2026: every post lands in the same band, and only a living painting held people

Findings:

- Every post lands at about 90 to 183 views. That is Instagram's first test audience for a small account; no post has broken out of it yet.
- Feature Reels are watched 2.4 to 5.4 s on average, of 30 to 55 s. The living painting inn-dawn was watched 19.3 s and got 9 likes. Steal this teaser Reels are watched 2.6 to 3.3 s. Carousels reach 7 to 14 views.

Decisions:

- Feature Reels stay as they are and are the backbone of the queue. Padding (Steal this, book nooks, picture-book scenes) never runs two days in a row.
- Under review: making living paintings the growth content at 2 to 3 a week. Decided on cup-town's numbers at 24 hours.

Experiments started:

- `songs-short`, a payoff-first 13 s cut that opens on Brilianto!'s picture as its song starts ("Step right up!"). Compare its views with the 90 to 180 band and its watch time with the features'.
- `cup-town`, posted 14 Nov at 23:05, a second living painting. It tells us whether inn-dawn's watch time was the format or luck.
