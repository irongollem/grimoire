If one of your players is under 16, they don't sign up on their own. A parent
or guardian sets up the account for them, or approves a request the young
player sends. That approval is what stands in for consent: nothing about the
account works until a parent has agreed to it.

A young player's account plays like any other: they can join campaigns, play
a character, chat and take notes. It cannot use AI features, buy anything, or
receive email, and it never appears anywhere with the child's real email
address, because it doesn't have one.

## Adding a young player from Account → Family

1. Open the account menu, then **Account**, then **Family** (or go straight to
   `/account/family`).
2. Click **Add a young player**.
3. Fill in a display name, a login name (3 to 30 lowercase letters, numbers or
   hyphens: this is what they'll type to sign in), and a password. You set
   both, and you can change the password later from the same page.
4. Answer the birth month and year question. Anyone under 16 gets a young
   player's account; 16 and over signs up for themselves instead.
5. Read the consent line and tick the box. It covers what the account can and
   can't do, and that characters and notes a young player writes are treated
   as ordinary game content, so a Dungeon Master running AI features in a
   campaign they play in may include them. Real photos of a real child still
   need your separate permission, and are never covered by this checkbox.
6. Click **Add young player**.

The new account shows up on the Family page straight away, with the login
name and the date it becomes theirs.

## Approving a request that arrived by email

Sometimes a young player gets there first: they try to sign up, or answer
"under 16" when Grimoire asks their age, and the app asks for a parent's
email address instead of letting them continue. You'll get an email with a
link. Open it while signed in to your own account (or sign in first if you
need to), and you'll land on the same "Add a young player" form, already
knowing whether it's a brand-new account or one the child already started,
and which campaign they were trying to join, if any. Fill in the login name
and password, tick the consent box, and submit exactly as above.

That link only works for the email address it was sent to, so make sure
you're signed in with that same address before you approve it. If the link
has expired, ask the young player to try again.

## Signing in with a login name

A young player's account signs in with the login name you chose, not an
email address. On the sign-in screen, the "Email or login name" field takes
either: type the login name there, and their password. There's nothing to
confuse them: whatever a family types is figured out automatically from
whether it looks like an email.

## What the account can't do

- **No AI.** Every AI tool is switched off for a young player's own account,
  whether they're playing or running their own campaign.
- **No purchases.** Grimoire Pro, credit packs and anything else that goes
  through checkout are unreachable.
- **No calendar feed.** A young DM's campaign can't publish the subscribable
  calendar link other campaigns can.
- **No email.** The account has no address of its own, so it never receives
  notification emails, and nobody can email it by mistake.

Everything else, playing a character, chatting, taking notes, running a
campaign on the free plan, works the same as any other account.

## Resetting the password, downloading data, deleting the account

All three live on the Family page, on the card for that young player:

- **Reset password** sets a new password immediately; there's no "forgot
  password" email flow for an account with no email.
- **Download their data** builds the same kind of export you can request for
  your own account: their profile, campaign memberships, journals, notes and
  preferences, and a list of files stored under their account.
- **Delete account** removes the account permanently. This is also how you
  withdraw consent: there's no separate "unlink" button, because once consent
  is withdrawn there's nothing left to manage. This cannot be undone.

You can't delete your own account while you still manage an active young
player's account. Remove theirs first, or wait until it becomes their own (see
below).

## What happens at 16

Nothing needs doing. From the first of the month after their 16th birthday,
the account stops counting as a young player's account and every restriction
lifts on its own. The account then behaves like any other: the next time they
sign in, Grimoire asks them to accept the current Terms of Service themselves,
the way any account does after a Terms update. It no longer appears on your
Family page.

## Adding a young player to your campaign

There's no separate step for this. Send them an ordinary invite link from
**Campaign Settings → Members & Invites**, the same way you would for any
player. Have them open it while signed in to their own account. If they
already have a young player's account, that's it, they join like anyone else.
If they don't have an account yet and try to sign up from the invite link,
Grimoire asks their age and routes them to the parent-request flow above,
carrying the invite along so approving the account also joins the campaign.

## Approving who your young player plays with

A young player can't simply drop into someone's campaign. When a join involves
a young player, it waits as a request until each parent it concerns has said
yes:

- **Your young player wants to join a campaign.** You are asked, unless you
  run that campaign yourself.
- **Someone wants to join your young player's campaign.** You are asked,
  unless that someone is you.
- **Both are young players.** Each parent is asked. If they are siblings, one
  yes from you covers both sides.

Requests waiting on you appear at the top of the Family page, under **Waiting
on you**. Each one says who wants to join which campaign and who runs it. Click
**Approve** to let them in, or **Decline** to turn the request down. A declined
player isn't told why and can ask again with the invite link. If the other
parent still has to answer, the request shows "You said yes. Waiting for the
other parent." until they do.

Until a request is approved, the player has no access to the campaign at all.

Each young player's card on the Family page also lists the campaigns they are
at, and who sits at each table. Young players are marked, and so are you. From
there you can take your young player out of any campaign they play in, and you
can remove anyone (except the person running it) from a campaign your young
player runs. Removing someone detaches their characters from the campaign but
doesn't delete them. The person who runs a campaign can't be removed here.
