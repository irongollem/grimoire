**Dungeon Features** are the interactive secrets embedded in your environments — things players can find and interact with if they look carefully enough. Find them under **Compendium → Dungeon Craft**, the **Features** tab (`/dungeon-craft`, the default tab).

## Key ideas

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Feature type | What the thing is — Secret Door, Hidden Cache, Moving Wall, etc. |
| Trigger | How it's activated or discovered |
| Glyph | How the feature draws as an icon on a Cartographer map |
| Placed In | The rooms this feature has been dropped into on your Atlas sites |

## Feature types

- **Secret Door** — a hidden door behind a bookcase, tapestry, or false wall.
- **Hidden Passage** — a concealed corridor or crawlspace.
- **Treasure Chest** — a chest hidden from plain sight.
- **Hidden Cache** — a stash of items concealed in a mundane location (loose floorboard, hollowed book).
- **Concealed Alcove** — a recess hidden behind a false wall or furniture.
- **Moving Wall** — a section of wall that slides, rotates, or descends.
- **Other** — any other secret worth noting.

## Trigger types

How the feature is activated or discovered: Lever, Pressure Plate, Bookshelf, Candlestick, Keyword, Puzzle, Key, Button/Knob, Magic Sensor, Combination, None, Other.

## Creating a feature

Click **New Feature** and fill in:

- **Name** — required.
- **Type** — from the list above.
- **How it draws on the map** — a **Glyph** picker: Secret Door, Hidden Passage, Cache, Moving Wall, Lever, Altar, Fountain, Statue, Rubble, Inscription, or leave it as the default **Generic feature marker**. This is what a Cartographer map shows wherever this feature is linked to a cell; it updates on every map the moment you change it.
- **Trigger** — what mechanism opens or reveals it.
- **Perception DC** — passive Perception (or active Perception check) DC to notice the feature.
- **Investigation DC** — Investigation check DC to identify how it works.
- **Arcana DC** — if the feature has a magical element, the Arcana DC to recognise it.
- **Contents / What's Inside** — rich text describing what's revealed once the feature is found or opened.
- **Description** — environmental context: how it blends in, what it looks like to a careful observer.
- **Tags**, **Image**, **DM Notes**.
- **Scope** — **General — all campaigns** or a specific campaign; new features default to your active campaign.

## Placing a feature in your world

A feature's own page has a **Placed In** panel showing every room it's dropped into across your Atlas sites, with an editable note and a picker to add a new placement — the same feature template (e.g. a stock "false-bottomed chest") can sit in several rooms. See [Sites — Maps, Rooms & Running a Dungeon](#sites-maps-rooms-running-a-dungeon) for the room-side view.

If a feature is a **Secret Door**, **Hidden Passage**, or **Moving Wall**, a door you draw on a site's Plan can cite it directly — the door then reads this feature's own DCs and trigger instead of you having to enter them twice. That's set up from the Atlas/Cartographer side; see [Sites — Maps, Rooms & Running a Dungeon](#sites-maps-rooms-running-a-dungeon).

## Usage during play

Dungeon Features are reference cards, not live-synced entities — they're for your own prep. Use them as:

- **Prep cards** — write all your secrets in advance and find them quickly during play.
- **Session notes** — after the session, note which features the party found or missed.

## What your players see

Dungeon Features have no player-facing view of their own — players never see a feature's card, DCs, or contents directly. What they experience is whatever you reveal at the table when they find it, or what the linked door/glyph shows on a published site map if you've made that room visible.

## Populate Examples

Click **Populate Examples** to seed a library of curated feature templates — classic dungeon secrets, trap-triggered features, magical wards, and more. Use them directly or modify them for your setting.
