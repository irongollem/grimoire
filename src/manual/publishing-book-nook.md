A **nook** is a place's picture that comes to life. You keep the painting, cut the things that should move out of it (a flame, a curtain, a cat), and Book Nook moves them a little, again and again, so the room seems to breathe. Nothing leaves the picture and nothing flashes: it is a quiet window into the room.

Find it under **Publish → Book Nook** in the sidebar (desktop only), at `/book-nook`.

## Making one from a place's picture

Open a place in the Atlas. If it has a picture, **Bring it to life** sits beside it. One click makes the nook and opens its studio, with the picture as the room. You can also press **New nook** in Book Nook and choose a place's picture, upload one, or have the room painted for you.

A place shows one nook, and a nook belongs to one place. Your nook saves itself as you go, so there is no Save button. **Done** takes you back to where you came from.

## The studio

The **layers** are on the left, the picture is in the middle, and whatever you have selected is on the right. Everything you add is a layer: the room at the bottom, then cut-outs, then touches such as glow, steam, smoke, dust, embers, rain, snow, fireflies and falling leaves.

Switch between **Front** and **Side** at the top. Front is the picture as people will see it: drag things into place. Side is like a toy theatre seen from the edge. Each thing is a card, and you drag it nearer or further away to set its depth. You never type a depth; a thing that stands on another shares its depth, so they stay together.

Each cut-out moves in a way named for what it does: **Flame**, **Cloth**, **Hanging**, **Floating** or **Wandering**. Pick one and set its **Strength** and **Pace**. If you want to tune the exact numbers, they are tucked under **Exact**.

Shadows belong to the light, not to the person looking. Mark a cut-out as casting a shadow and choose the light it comes from. Near a flame the shadow falls away from the light and wavers with the flicker. It never stretches just because someone tilts their phone.

Along the top you set the picture as a whole: the **loop** (every motion repeats exactly in it), **breathing** (Still, Gentle or Deep), a **mood**, and an optional **frame**. A frame is art that covers the edge of the screen when the picture is full screen. It never moves; the room moves behind it.

**Show on** lets you check the picture in each window: TV, Table, Atlas, and a tall one for phones. Book Nook warns you if the picture would show a dark edge in one of them, or if a cut-out's edge would float in view, and offers a gentler setting.

## Getting art

Press **Add a cut-out** in the layers (or **Add a touch** for glow, steam and the like). There are four ways, and the first two are free.

- **Stock shelf**: ready-made cut-outs and frames painted for Grimoire. More arrive as they are finished.
- **Upload**: a PNG or WebP with a transparent background. If your picture has a flat background, you are offered **Remove background**, which costs one picture.
- **Lift**: draw a box round something in the room and Book Nook cuts it out and paints in the gap behind it, so the wall is whole when it moves. Lift costs two pictures, because it makes the cut-out and then fills the gap.
- **Paint**: describe a thing and it is painted to match the room's light, on a transparent background. This costs one picture.

The price is shown on each button before you press it.

## Where it shows

- **On the place**: the nook plays in the Atlas on the place's Overview, in place of the still picture.
- **On your players' phones**: they see it on the place when you share it. Tapping it opens it full screen, and on a phone they can tilt the device to look round the room (an iPhone asks permission the first time; if they say no, the picture simply keeps breathing). On a computer the pointer does the same.
- **On the Table screen**: the picture shows when the place has no map to play on. When it does have a map, use **On the table** on the explore bar to switch between Picture and Map. Your choice is kept with the party's position. During a fight the Table screen always shows the map.

If someone has reduced motion turned on, they see the still picture instead of the moving one.
