A **nook** is a picture of a room that comes to life. You keep the painting, cut the things that should move out of it (a flame, a curtain, a cat), and Book Nook moves them a little, again and again, so the room seems to breathe. Nothing leaves the picture and nothing flashes: it is a quiet window into the room.

Find it under **Publish → Book Nook** in the sidebar (desktop only), at `/book-nook`.

## Making one from a location's picture

Open a location in the Atlas. If it has a picture, **Bring it to life** sits beside it. One click makes the nook and opens its studio, with the picture as the room. You can also press **New nook** in Book Nook and start **From the Atlas**, **Upload** a picture of your own, or, when the campaign has AI on, **Paint with AI**. Painting makes one picture, the empty room, for the credits its button shows. Everything after that is free unless its button shows a price: only painting or lifting a cut-out costs credits.

A location shows one nook, and a nook belongs to one location. Your nook saves itself as you go, so there is no Save button. **Done** takes you back to where you came from.

## The studio

The **layers** are on the left, the picture is in the middle, and whatever you have selected is on the right. Everything you add is a layer: the room at the bottom, then cut-outs, then touches such as glow, steam, smoke, dust, embers, rain, snow, fireflies and falling leaves.

Switch between **Front** and **Side** at the top. Front is the picture as people will see it: drag things into place. Side is like a toy theatre seen from the edge. Each thing is a card, and you drag it nearer or further away to set its depth. You never type a depth; a thing that stands on another shares its depth, so they stay together.

Each cut-out moves in a way named for what it does: **Flame**, **Cloth**, **Hanging**, **Floating** or **Wandering**. Pick one and set its **Strength** and **Pace**. If you want to tune the exact numbers, they are tucked under **Exact**.

Shadows belong to the light, not to the person looking. Mark a cut-out as casting a shadow and choose the light it comes from. Near a flame the shadow falls away from the light and wavers with the flicker. It never stretches just because someone tilts their phone.

Along the top you set the picture as a whole: the **loop** (every motion repeats exactly in it), **breathing** (Still, Gentle or Deep), a **mood**, and an optional **frame**. A frame is art that covers the edge of the screen when the picture is full screen. It never moves; the room moves behind it.

Once the nook is shown in the Atlas, a **Sound** row appears under these settings. A nook's sound is its location's ambience, so changing it here changes the location, and the other way round. Leave it blank and the location uses its parent's sound; the field shows which. Press the note to hear it. It keeps playing after you leave the studio, as it does when you press Play on the location. Nothing plays just because you opened the nook.

**Show on** lets you check the picture in each window: TV, Table, Atlas, and a tall one for phones. Book Nook warns you if the picture would show a dark edge in one of them, or if a cut-out's edge would float in view, and offers a gentler setting.

## Getting art

Press **Add a cut-out** in the layers (or **Add a touch** for glow, steam and the like). There are four ways, and the first two are free.

- **Stock shelf**: ready-made cut-outs and frames painted for Grimoire. More arrive as they are finished.
- **Upload**: a PNG or WebP with a transparent background. If your picture has a flat background, you are offered **Remove background**, which costs one picture.
- **Lift**: paint over something in the room (hold Alt, or pick Eraser, to take paint away; zoom in with Ctrl and the wheel, or pinch, to paint the edges), and say what it is if your paint covers more than one thing, such as "the music box, not the table". Book Nook cuts it out and paints in the gap behind it, so the wall is whole when it moves. Lift costs two pictures, because it makes the cut-out and then fills the gap.
- **Paint**: describe a thing and it is painted to match the room's light, on a transparent background. This costs one picture.

The price is shown on each button before you press it.

## Where it shows

- **In the Atlas**: the nook plays on the location's Overview, instead of its still picture.
- **On your players' phones**: they see it on the location when you share it. Tapping it opens it full screen, and on a phone they can tilt the device to look round the room (an iPhone asks permission the first time; if they say no, the picture simply keeps breathing). On a computer the pointer does the same.
- **On the Table screen**: the picture shows when the location has no map to play on. When it does have a map, use **On the table** on the explore bar to switch between Picture and Map. Your choice is kept with the party's position. During a fight the Table screen always shows the map.

If someone has reduced motion turned on, they see the still picture instead of the moving one.
