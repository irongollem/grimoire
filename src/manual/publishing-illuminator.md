The **Illuminator** is the picture editor inside the [Scriptorium](#scriptorium-document-publisher). You use it on a picture in your book, without leaving the book. It has no page of its own in the sidebar.

Edges, colour, vignette, texture, focus and the brush all run on your own device at full resolution. Only the AI actions (see below) use a server.

## Opening it

Select a picture on the page. The panel beside the page shows **Edit picture** with a few quick choices:

- **Edges**: Torn paper, Torn (classic), Faded or Straight, with the sides you want to tear and sliders for the tear.
- **Colour**: a preset, plus **Warmth** and **Contrast**.
- **Vignette**.
- **Paper wash**: **Paint a paper wash** (see Wash below).

Press **More tools: open the Illuminator** for everything else. The Illuminator opens over the book. **Done, back to page N** returns you to the same page with your edits in place. **Cancel** drops the session.

You can edit a picture in the text and a picture placed on the page in the same way. Any picture stored in Grimoire can be edited: one you uploaded, a portrait, a place's art or a library picture. A picture linked from another website cannot.

Press **Crop** beside **Edit picture…** to open the Illuminator straight on the Crop tool. See Crop below.

Under **Paper**, choose **Printed into the paper** to let the paper's colour and grain show through the picture, as if it were printed on the page, or **On top** to show it as it is.

## Key ideas

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Original** | The picture as you first added it. It always stays in the book, untouched. |
| **Edits** | Your choices, kept as settings over the original. The book prints from these, so a stale copy never reaches the PDF. |
| **Reset** | **Reset to the original picture** in the panel clears all edits. It always works. |
| **Before and after** | A slider on the stage that compares the original with your edits. |

## Tools

Choose a tool from the rail on the left. Its controls are on the right. The rail has two groups. **Paint** holds Brush, Smudge, Eraser and Pick colour. **Picture** holds Crop, Edges, Grade, Vignette, Texture, Wash and Focus. Each tile shows its key in the corner (see the keys below), and hovering names it. A tool called **Text** is greyed out and marked as coming later. The Illuminator opens on the tool you used last.

| Tool | Key |
| --- | --- |
| Brush, Smudge, Eraser, Pick colour | B, S, E, I |
| Crop, Edges, Grade | C, R, G |
| Vignette, Texture, Wash, Focus | V, T, W, F |

**P** shows the before-and-after slider. It is switched off while Crop and Focus are active.

### Crop

Trims the picture to the part you want to keep. Every other edit then works on the part you keep, and the picture on the page and in the PDF takes the new shape. The original is never cut.

1. Choose **Crop** in the rail, or press **Crop** in the panel beside the page.
2. Drag the box, or its handles, over the part you want to keep.
3. To lock the box to a shape, choose one under **Shape**: **Free**, **1:1**, **4:3**, **3:2** or **16:9**. A picture placed on the page also has **Page**, which matches the shape of the page so the picture fills it. A picture in the text has no Page shape.
4. The panel tells you what you keep, for example "Keeping 80% across and 60% down." It reads "Keeping the whole picture." when nothing is cut.
5. **Reset crop** puts the whole picture back.

### Grade

Colour grading for the picture: the mood of its colours, not the colour you paint with. Its panel is headed **Colour grading** and has a switch to turn it on or off.

- **Presets**: **Sepia**, **Moonlit**, **Vivid** and **Ink sketch**. A preset sets the sliders for you, and you can adjust them afterwards.
- Sliders: **Brightness**, **Contrast**, **Saturation**, **Temp** (warm or cool) and **Hue**.
- **Reset** puts the sliders back to neutral.

### Wash

Paints the page's own paper over part of the picture, so the picture runs to the edge of the page and the text sits on a brushed patch of paper. Where the brush went, the picture is erased and the paper shows through, with a dry-brush edge where the painting shows faintly through. It shows on screen and in the PDF.

1. Choose **Wash** in the rail, or find **Paper wash** in the panel beside the page.
2. Press **Paint a paper wash**.
3. Under **Paper comes in from**, choose the side: **Left**, **Right**, **Top** or **Bottom**.
4. Set **How far it reaches**, **Brush (fine to broad)** and **Edge (wet to dry)**.
5. Press **Paint it again** for a different brushing with the same settings. **Remove the wash** takes it away.

### Edges

A torn, rough or faded edge. Choose a style and which sides it touches (Top, Right, Bottom, Left).

- **Torn paper**: an irregular torn outline with paper fibres and a soft lift shadow. Sliders: **Roughness**, **Tear depth**, **Fibres** and **Lift shadow**.
- **Torn (classic)**: the layered torn fade the Illuminator has always had, with its own sliders for each side.
- **Faded** and **Straight**.

### Vignette

Darkens or fades the corners. **Type** is Transparent (fades to nothing) or Colour (fades to a colour you choose). **Strength** and **Softness** set how far it reaches.

### Texture

Upload a tiling texture such as parchment, stone or fabric. Choose a **Blend mode**, then set **Opacity** and **Tile scale**.

### Focus

Click the picture to set the sharp spot. Everything away from it blurs. Choose the **Falloff** (Linear, Quadratic or Cubic) and set **Focus radius**, **Blur strength** and **Desaturation**, which slightly drains the colour from blurred areas.

### Painting

The **Paint** group at the top of the tool rail lets you paint on the picture itself: **Brush** (B), **Smudge** (S), **Eraser** (E) and **Pick colour** (I). Below them, the two colour squares show the colour you paint with in front and the background colour behind. **X** swaps them and **D** returns to ink on paper.

- **Brush** lays paint down as a stroke. Along the bar above the stage you set the **Size**, **Opacity**, **Flow** and **Smoothing**, a **Blend mode**, and open the brush library to choose a brush.
- **Smudge** smears the paint already on a layer.
- **Eraser** takes paint away. On the picture itself it erases the picture, so the page's paper shows through.
- **Pick colour** takes the colour from anywhere on the stage.
- Only a pen reports pressure. A mouse, trackpad or finger paints at full strength.

Your paint goes onto its own **layers**, above the picture, and the picture underneath is never painted on. The right-hand panel has **Brushes**, **Layers** and **Colour** sections you can open together. In **Layers** you can add a **New layer**, rename it, set its blend mode and **Opacity**, **Lock transparency**, **Merge down**, **Delete**, or **Flatten into the picture**. One undo history covers every layer.

#### Your own brushes

Under **Yours** in the brush library, choose **New brush...** to make a brush four ways: **From this brush** (keep the one you have tuned), **From a photo of a mark**, **From a box on the picture** (drag a box round a mark you painted), or **Describe it** (AI, if your campaign has it on): type a few words such as "a wet sponge dab", and four tips come back to choose from. **Import brushes...** reads brush files from other painting programs, and tells you afterwards what it could not carry over. A brush of yours saves its settings by itself.

#### Paint on a whole page

You can also paint straight on a page without starting from a picture. In the Scriptorium panel, under **Add to this page**, click **Paint on this page**. A transparent painting the size of the page is placed behind the text, and the Illuminator opens on it with the Brush ready. Your paper shows through wherever you have not painted, on screen and in print. **Done** keeps it. **Cancel** straight after starting takes the empty painting away again.

Free accounts keep one painting with layers. **Flatten into the picture** frees its slot, or Pro has no limit.

**Reset all to defaults** at the foot of the right column clears every tool.

## Describe it: a picture painted from words

If AI is on for the campaign, you can have a picture painted for your book instead of finding one.

1. Select a picture on the page, and in its panel click **Describe it…**. It is shown only for a book of the campaign you have open, with AI on.
2. In **Describe the picture**, write the **Scene prompt**. Type **@** and a name to bring in an NPC, a character or a place, and an NPC with a portrait is painted to match it. The **Resolved characters** list shows which names were understood.
3. Choose a **Shape**: **Full page**, **Tall**, **Square**, **Landscape**, **Wide banner** or **Narrow column**. Each shows its credit cost.
4. Click **Generate**. The dialog closes and you can keep writing: the picture paints in the background and swaps itself in when it is ready. While it paints, the button reads **Painting…**. If it fails, you are told.

The picture arrives as a normal picture you can edit with the quick edits or the Illuminator. It carries the AI label wherever it is shown, and if you change the shape of the crop later, the generated file itself is left untouched.

## Ask the Illuminator (AI)

If AI is turned on for your campaign, the workbench also offers **Ask the Illuminator**. Each action carries the sparkle mark and shows what it costs in credits:

- **Cut out the figure**: the figure alone, good for placing behind the text.
- **Turn into an ink sketch** and **Paint as a watercolour**: restyle the picture and keep its composition.
- **Extend to the page edge**: grows the picture toward the edge you choose.
- **Match this book's look**: restyles the picture to suit the book's look.

The result becomes the picture you edit from, so you can add an edge or colour on top. It is labelled AI-made wherever it is shown. If a restyle could show a real person, you are asked to confirm first. **Use the original picture** goes back to the first picture, and asks first because the result cost credits.

If AI is off for the campaign, none of this appears.

## Saving a copy

**Save as a new picture** in the header has three choices: **Download as PNG**, **Copy to the clipboard** and **Add to my pictures**. The picture in the book stays as it is.

## Tips

- **Your first picture is safe.** Edits are settings over the original, so you can change your mind at any time.
- **Edges are seeded, not random.** A small preview and the full-size picture tear the same way.
- **A big edit can take a moment to settle.** The page shows a quick preview first and the full-quality picture follows in the background.
