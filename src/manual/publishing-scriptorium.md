The **Scriptorium** produces print-quality campaign materials (adventure modules, spell compendiums, monster bestiaries, handouts, session recaps) that look like an actual rulebook, not a text export. Find it in the sidebar under **Compendium → Publish → Scriptorium** (route `/scriptorium`). Writing needs a tablet or desktop screen. On a phone, Scriptorium is a reader; see "On a phone" below.

You write directly on the finished, paginated page. The page you see is the page that prints: page breaks, columns, footers and pictures are where they will be on paper. The Scriptorium owns pagination, columns, fonts and page numbering, so you never lay those out by hand.

## Key ideas

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Template** | A finished-looking starting book you pick before you write anything: never a blank page. |
| **The page** | The sheet you write on. It is the real page, so there is no separate preview to look at. |
| **The panel** | The column beside the page. It describes what you have selected, or the page itself when nothing is selected. |
| **Book kit** | A tab beside Chapters with every block, your campaign's entries and your pictures. Drag them onto the page. |
| **Read as spreads** | The whole book full screen as facing pages, in lamplight, with room for your own notes. A button in the header. |
| **Look** | How the book is set: **Grimoire** (the default for new books), **2024 book** or **2014 book**. A handout is set in a theme (Letter, Notice, Journal page or Plain page) instead. |
| **Paper** | What the pages are printed on, for a book or a handout: the twelve papers the painting tool starts from, or the look's own. Set in the book's or handout's settings. Ink-friendly prints plain white. |
| **Page dressing** | Hand-painted marks on every page, a little different on each: a darker stain low on the page, a wash along the outer edge, and flourishes in the corners. Tick the parts you want and set how strong each is in the book's or handout's settings; **Shuffle** moves the marks and keeps your choices. Covers and blank pages stay bare, and ink-friendly prints none. |
| **Furniture** | A decoration (watercolor splatter, watermark, artist credit) or a picture placed on the page, independent of the text. Drag it directly on the page. |
| **Illuminator** | The picture editor that opens over the book. See "Editing a picture". |
| **Doc type** | Custom, Spell, Monster, Item, Class, Subclass, Species, Background, Adventure, NPC Sheet, Location, or Quest: colour-codes the document in your list. |

## Starting a document

1. Click **Create your first document** (or **New**) from the document list.
2. Pick a **template**: Blank Book, Adventure Module, Monster Compendium, Spell Compendium, Subclass Supplement, or One-Page Dungeon, each seeded with real cover, heading, and table structure for that kind of document. Every template is fully editable afterward.
3. Or click **Import Markdown** to bring an existing `.md` file (chapters, notes, even a Homebrewery brew) in as your starting book instead of a template.

### Drafting from your campaign with AI

If AI is turned on for your campaign, the template gallery and the Scriptorium list both offer **Draft with AI**. It writes a first draft of one of three documents from your own campaign data:

- **Player handout**: an in-world letter, notice, journal page or proclamation about an NPC, a location or a faction.
- **Faction dossier**: a sectioned briefing on one faction, with its leadership, members, holdings, goals and relations.
- **Session recap**: a past-tense recap of one session, written from the players' side and ending with the open threads.

Choose the kind, pick the NPC, location, faction or session it is about, and say who it is for. Add a steer if you like ("a hurried letter, half burnt"). The draft is grounded in the rows it is about and the things linked to them, and it never contradicts them. When it is written for **Players**, only what your players can already see is used, so DM-only notes and secrets stay out. When it is for **DM only**, everything on record is used.

Drafting costs credits, and the cost shows beside the button. The result opens as a normal, fully editable document with plain headings and paragraphs, ready for you to restyle. Once you change the text, the document is no longer marked as untouched AI output.


## Writing

The toolbar above the page has the tools you reach for most:

- **Text**: **B** (bold), **I** (italic), a **Heading** menu (Paragraph, Heading 1, 2 or 3) and **List**.
- **Read-aloud**: a boxed passage to read to your players.
- **Sidebar**: a callout box.
- **Stat block**: a stat block to fill in.
- **Table**.
- **Drop cap**: sets the first letter of the paragraph you are in large. Press it again to take it off. The words do not change.
- **Wide**: makes a block run across both columns.
- **From the campaign**: opens the Book kit with its search ready, to bring in a linked NPC, monster, spell, item, quest or place.
- **More**: strikethrough, inline code, numbered list, callout, code block and page break.
- **Cover**, **Undo** and **Redo**.

Everything saves as you write. There is no Save button.

The details of the book are in **Book settings** (the gear in the header): the title, whether it is a book or a handout, the campaign, the look, the page size, two columns, ink-friendly printing, page numbers, the running title in the footer, whether it is Published, and tags. Ink-friendly strips backgrounds and decorations for cheaper printing.

### The Book kit and the slash menu

Open the **Book kit** tab beside **Chapters** to drag a block, a monster, an NPC, a place, a spell, an item, a quest or a picture onto the page. A gold line shows where it will land. A click puts it at your cursor instead. Blocks land between other blocks, never inside a box.

The kit has three parts:

- **Blocks**: the common ones as tiles (read-aloud, sidebar, stat block, table and more), and **More blocks** for the rest, such as cover pages, spacers, the contents page and the class tables.
- **From the campaign**: search your campaign's NPCs, monsters, spells, items, quests, places, deities, species, factions and subclasses.
- **Art**: browse your pictures, paste a web address (http or https only), or reuse a picture you picked earlier in this session.

Type **/** on an empty line to insert any block without leaving the keyboard. Keep typing to narrow the list, then press Enter.

### Boxes and tables

Select a read-aloud, sidebar, quote, wide block or table and the panel shows **Block**. It tells you which look the block wears (change the book's look in Book settings and every block follows). Two choices are yours:

- **Keep on one page**: the page never splits the block. If it does not fit, it moves to the next page whole.
- **Across both columns**: runs the block over both columns. It shows only in a two-column book.

### The panel and the header

On a wide screen the panel is always there. On a narrower one it slides in when you select something and goes away when you click back into the text or press its close button.

- **Focus** in the header hides the chapter list and the panel so the page has all the room. Press it again to bring them back.
- **Zoom** sits beside it: minus, the level, and plus. Choose **Fit width** (Cmd or Ctrl 0) to fill the space, or **Actual size** (Cmd or Ctrl 1) for the printed size. The steps are 50, 75, 100, 125, 150 and 200 percent. Your choice is remembered on this device.
- **Read as spreads** shows the whole book as facing pages. Escape or Close brings you back to the page, and a note's **Edit this part** takes you to its text.

While a long book is still being numbered, the chapter list says so and shows how far it has got. You can keep writing.

### Covers, the contents page and footers

These are drawn on the page as they will print, and you edit them where they are.

- **Covers.** Type the title and the other words straight onto the cover. The panel becomes **Cover** and holds the art (upload or browse your library), where the title sits (Top, Middle or Bottom), the **Shade under the title** (None, Dusk or Parchment, once art is set on a front cover), and which cover it is (Front, Inside, Part or Back). A part divider has a part number. A back cover has three blurbs, a tagline and a product link. Changes show as you make them, and there is nothing to apply.
- **The contents page.** It is made from your headings and updates as you write. To change an entry, change its heading. Select it and the panel says so.
- **Footers.** Every page shows its footer in the bottom margin. Click one and the panel becomes **Footer**. There you choose the running title for right pages (Chapter, Book or Custom), whether to show page numbers and where the count starts. Left pages always carry the book's title. Three choices apply to that page only: **No footer on this page**, **Don't count this page** and **Start the count again here**.

### Pictures

Select a picture and the panel becomes **Picture**. Its **Layer** control says where the picture lives: **In the text** (it moves as you write), **Behind the text** or **In front** (placed on the page).

In the text you choose a size (S, M, L or XL), whether it aligns left, centre or right, and whether the text wraps around it. When it wraps, you also choose whether it bleeds into the column gutter and whether it stays **with its text** in print.

To put a picture anywhere on a page, choose **Behind the text** or **In front**. It stays where it was, and now you can drag it and resize it from its corner, on the page you write on. Then choose whether it **moves with its paragraph** or **stays on its page**. When a picture moves with its paragraph, it keeps its place beside it as the text grows, and a small mark in the left margin shows which paragraph it follows. Drag the mark onto another paragraph to attach the picture there. If a change in your text carries the picture to another page, the page tells you.

You can also pin a picture to one of the page's four corners, flush against both paper edges. Drag it near a corner and it snaps there. Behind the text, a picture is printed into the paper at a **Strength** you set, so the words over it stay readable. Choose **In the text** again and it goes back into the writing, right after its paragraph, with any edits it had.

A picture behind the text is picked up by clicking empty space over it, or by the small **Picture** handle that appears when you point at it. A click on a word over it still puts your cursor in the text.

**Replace**, **Upload a file**, **Edit picture** and **Remove** are below the layer choices.

When a wrapped picture does not fit at the foot of a column, the printer can move it on and leave its text behind. The page then shows a note beside the picture with two choices: **Keep it with its text**, so the two move together, or **Place it on the page instead**.

### Editing a picture

Pictures from your own library can be edited without leaving the book. With a picture selected, the panel shows **Edit picture** with a few quick choices:

- **Edges**: Torn paper, Torn (classic), Faded or Straight, with the sides you want and sliders for the tear.
- **Colour**: a preset, plus **Warmth** and **Contrast**.
- **Vignette**.

For more, press **More tools: open the Illuminator**. It opens over the book with a rail of tools and a before and after slider. **Done** brings you back to the same page. The [Illuminator](#illuminator-image-effects) page lists every tool.

Your edits are kept as settings over the original picture, so **Reset to the original picture** always works. A copy of the original stays in the book and is what prints.

If AI is turned on for your campaign, the Illuminator also offers AI picture actions, marked with the sparkle. They cost credits and are labelled AI-made.

### Linked entities

An NPC, monster, spell, item, location or quest brought in from the Book kit (or the **Send to Scriptorium** button on that entity's own page) is a **live link**, not a one-time snapshot. It always shows that entity's current data, so editing the NPC later updates every book that uses it, with nothing to insert again. The entry is drawn on the page as it will print. Hover it to see two controls:

- **Open** jumps straight to that entity's own page.
- **Detach** turns the block into ordinary editable text, frozen at its current content. Use this when you want to diverge from the source, for example a tougher version of a stat block for one fight. Detaching cannot be undone from the block itself (the usual Undo still works), so it asks you to confirm first.

If the source entity is later deleted, the block shows a plain "no longer available" notice instead of stale or blank content, both on the page and in the exported book.

A linked **monster** or **NPC** is a full entry: name, framed stat block, art and lore, laid out as a column-width block or a page-wide band depending on how much the stat block holds. Select it and the panel becomes **Linked entry**. There you choose a size (Auto, One column or Full width), **Show the art** with a cutout or picture choice, **Show the lore**, whether a wide entry's band sits at the top or bottom, what sharing the handout reveals, and **Starts a new page** (on by default, so each entry starts fresh like a Monster Manual page; turn it off for a variant that should share its family's first entry).

### Decorating a page

Watercolor splatters, watermarks and artist credits are **furniture**: they sit on the page independent of the flowing text. Select the page, then use **Add to this page** in the panel. Drag a decoration on the page to move it. Select it and the panel becomes **Decoration**, where you set its look, its layer (behind or in front of the text), its width and its corner, or remove it.

## Preview and export

For the whole book at once, press **Read as spreads** in the header. It fits a facing pair of pages to your screen and moves through the book a spread at a time with the arrow keys.

Click **Export PDF** (or **Print** for a handout) to download a real PDF, with selectable text, embedded fonts and pictures. A dialog follows the work, and you can keep writing while a big book prints in parts. The file is saved to your downloads. There is no print dialog to click through.

After the file is saved, the Scriptorium reads it back and checks every page against the page you were looking at. The dialog says "All 28 pages printed as laid out" when they did. If a page printed differently, it names the page and a **Show me** button takes you there. The check never holds up your download.

For a document that belongs to a campaign, the export dialog also lets you attach campaign data: pick the NPCs, monsters and other entities that travel inside the file. See [Sharing Adventures as PDFs](#sharing-adventures-as-pdfs).

## The document list

Your document list shows a card grid with a colour-coded type bar, word count and the Published badge. Filter by **type**, **scope** and free-text **search**, all remembered across visits so navigating away and back does not reset them.

The **Published** setting in Book settings marks a document with a green badge in your list. It is a DM-only status flag for tracking what is done and what is draft. It does not share the document with players. To do that, use **Give to players** (see below).

### Giving a handout to players

A handout belongs to a campaign. **Share** in the header (or **Give to players** on a handout) lets you hand it to chosen party members, or pin it on a notice board. Before anything is shared, you see what it will reveal (for example an NPC's name) and what players will not see. Each linked entry decides its own reveal, which you set in the panel's **Linked entry** section. A banner tells you when a linked entry would still be hidden from the people who hold the handout.

### Campaign scope

Every document belongs to one campaign, or to none. A new document defaults to your active campaign; the **Campaign** setting in Book settings lets you switch it to **All my campaigns** instead, which makes it account-wide and visible no matter which campaign you have open. A document you wrote while a different campaign was active keeps showing that campaign's name there, so saving it never quietly moves it somewhere else.

The document list's **Scope** filter finds documents by the same classification: *Usable here* (the default: your active campaign's documents plus your account-wide ones), *This campaign*, *General* (account-wide only), or *Other campaigns* to find something you wrote while working in a different campaign. Deleting a campaign never deletes the documents scoped to it; they become account-wide instead.

A document scoped to a campaign can only be attached as a quest handout within that same campaign (or an account-wide one): attaching from the quest beat's Placements panel already narrows the picker this way.


## On a phone

The document list and its filters work on a phone exactly as they do everywhere else, but writing does not: the page layout, art placement, and formatting tools need more room than a phone gives them, so **New Document** is hidden there and opening one for editing shows a short explanation with a way back to the list instead.

Tapping a document from the list instead opens it as a **reader**: a single flowing column, like an e-book, rather than the paginated book. Page and column breaks disappear (a page break leaves a faint divider), a cover page becomes a compact card at the top with its title and art, back-cover blurbs become a closing card, images run the full width of the screen, and tables scroll sideways inside their own frame so they never widen the page. If the document has any headings, a tappable **Contents** list sits above the text and jumps straight to the section you tap. These are the same headings the printed book's contents page uses, including a linked monster or NPC's own entry name.


## Tips

- **Templates are a starting point, not a lock-in.** Change the look, the page size or the columns any time after picking one.
- **Furniture and text are independent layers.** Moving a watercolor splatter never reflows your text, and editing text never moves a decoration.
- **Zoom out to see a whole page.** Fit width is a good default for writing. Use Actual size when you want to judge type.
