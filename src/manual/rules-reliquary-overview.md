The **Reliquary** (**Compendium → Reliquary** in the sidebar, `/rules`) is where the game's rules live: a quick-reference screen, the full SRD text, your own house rules, this manual, and the licence attributions for everything Grimoire hosts. It also includes a built-in dice roller. Players get a matching, read-only version in their own portal.

## Key ideas

| Tab | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| DM Screen | A static, always-available quick-reference — the tables you'd keep on a physical screen |
| Compendium | The full SRD text, browsable or searchable |
| Custom Rules | Your own house rules, tables, and mechanical trackers |
| DM Manual | This manual |
| Licenses | Attribution for every shared-content publisher and audio catalogue Grimoire hosts |

## The five tabs

1. **DM Screen** — see [DM Screen](#dm-screen).
2. **Compendium** — see [SRD Compendium](#srd-compendium).
3. **Custom Rules** — see [Custom Rules (House Rules)](#custom-rules-house-rules) and [Custom Rule Trackers](#custom-rule-trackers).
4. **DM Manual** — the page you're reading right now. Search it from the sidebar on the left, or jump straight to a page from the ⓘ help button next to most screens in the app.
5. **Licenses** — per-source attribution for every body of shared content in Grimoire's database (monsters, spells, items, species, rules, classes) and for the soundboard's audio catalogue, grouped by licence. Most of what's hosted here is **not** WotC SRD — of roughly 3,500 shared monsters only about 660 are — so this tab exists to keep publisher and licence correctly attached to the content you're using, not just to the app as a whole. It also renders the full OGL 1.0a text where OGL-licensed content is present.

## Tips

- All three searchable tabs (Compendium, Custom Rules, and this Manual) keep their search box contents while you switch tabs or leave the Reliquary entirely — coming back picks up where you left off.
- If you're not sure whether a rule belongs in **Custom Rules** or is just something you want at a glance, the DM Screen already covers the standard SRD tables (conditions, actions, DCs, rests, and more) — Custom Rules is for the rulings and systems that are yours.
