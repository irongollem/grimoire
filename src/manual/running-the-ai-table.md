The AI table lets you play a character in your own campaign while an AI **Narrator** is the Dungeon Master. You say what your character says and does, the Narrator tells what happens, and the people you meet answer in their own boxes. It is a **Beta**, and it is not open to everyone yet.

## Is it open for you?

The **Table** entry in the Campaign group of the menu (marked Beta) appears only when two things are true: AI is switched on for the campaign, and the table has been enabled for the account that owns the campaign. It is released to chosen accounts for now. If you do not see **Table**, the table is not enabled for you yet. A campaign that plays no 5e system, so has no characters to play, does not offer it either.

## Starting a sitting

Open **Table**. The page, called "The table", says "Play a character while an AI Narrator tells the story."

- **Before you sit down** holds the settings you may want first: the Narrator's voice, whether the characters talk among themselves in a conversation, and the fight options (see below). You can change them later at the table.
- **Who you play** is required: pick one of the party's characters.
- **Where you begin** is optional.
- **Sit down** begins.

The Narrator opens the scene itself: a few sentences about what your character sees and who is here. It decides nothing for you.

**Prep campaign** on this page has the AI propose the hazards, places, items, roll tables and dungeon features your quests will need, so the Narrator has something to work with.

The first time, you are shown a short note that the Narrator is an AI (**The Narrator is an AI**); accept it to go on.

### Where a campaign begins

You can say which quest a campaign starts with: on the quest, tick **The campaign begins with this quest**. The very first sitting then starts at that quest's first beat, and the Narrator reads the beat's read-aloud in your story. The page says "Where the story begins: <quest>" instead of asking for a place. Once you have a sitting, the story is picked up where you left it, so this is not read again.

### Picking up later

You do not start over. If you never left a sitting, the table reopens it. After you leave, the page offers **Pick up the story** as the same character at the place the party was left. **Play someone else, or begin somewhere else** is behind it. Earlier sittings are listed so you can read them again.

## Playing

You play as a player: the DM's menu steps out of the way, and you see only what your character has found out (their journal, people, bestiary and sheet). **The Dungeon Master's side** steps out without ending the sitting.

- **Say** speaks as your character. **Do** describes what they do. **Ask the Narrator** asks a question outside the story. **Go on** says and does nothing, and lets things happen.
- **That's not what happened** lets you correct the Narrator: write what is true, and **Set it right**.
- **Stop** ends an answer in progress and keeps what was said.
- Beside the story you see **Where you are**, **Here with you** and **What you are after** (the quests your character knows about). On a phone these are tabs: Story, Sheet and Scene.
- Your **Sheet** shows hit points (with Damage and Heal), armor class, speed, conditions, spell slots and skills, and links to the full sheet.

### When the roll is yours

The Narrator never rolls for your character. When a check or save is needed, **Your roll** appears above the box with the name of the check and your bonus. Press **Roll it here**, or roll your own dice at the table and type the face under **I rolled a**. Choose **Normal**, **Advantage** or **Disadvantage**. A condition that forces disadvantage is shown and combined with your choice. The difficulty is never shown to you. Rolls the Narrator makes in the open are marked "Rolled by Grimoire"; secret ones are never shown.

### Fights, journeys and the world

When the story turns into a fight, it opens as a fight with a map, a roster and your turn (**Your turn**, **Your reaction**, **End my turn**). Characters with you take their turns too. Before the Narrator tells a journey it checks your campaign's own roads, so distances and days come from your world, and days that pass move your campaign's calendar. What happens on the way is the Narrator's call: a chance meeting, a camp, or nothing.

### Voices

Lines can be read aloud, in a man's or a woman's Narrator voice, one voice for the whole table. Under **Read aloud** you can switch voices on if your server has them. A voiced line costs more than a text one.

## What it costs

A turn costs what the Narrator used to answer it: more when it looks things up, less for a short reply. You are charged one whole credit each time the running total passes one. **This sitting** shows what the sitting has cost. The two checkboxes **Let the characters talk among themselves** and **Foes speak in a fight** make the table livelier and cost a turn each time they speak. **Helpers act only when something changes** saves credits in a fight.

## Keeping it a game

The table is a D&D table: violence and horror are told as a D&D table tells them, and nothing sexual is told. A scene that would go there is closed inside the story, and a message that tries is refused and costs nothing. An account that keeps trying finds the table closed. You can turn violence and horror up or down for the campaign under **Violence and horror at the AI table** in the campaign settings, Details tab: **Gentle**, **Standard** or **Grim**.

## Leaving the table

Press **Leave the table** in the bar. Starting a sitting opens the campaign's play session, and leaving ends it, but only if the sitting started it. If you were already running a session, yours is left alone.

When there is something to record, a session note titled "At the AI table" is written, with the facts the party now knows. A sitting you left can be read again but not continued.

## The story so far

**The story so far** is the record of what the table settled, kept as plain facts you can read: grouped by session, with links to the people, places and quests they are about. Players can always read it. It is open even when the table is not.

As the DM you can:

- **Correct this**: the old fact stays, struck through, and the correction follows it.
- **Add to the record** a fact by hand.
- **Reveal** a fact the party does not know yet.

## What play reveals

As you move through the story, your journal, people and bestiary fill in. The place you stand in and the people who spoke in front of you are added to your character's view, and monsters join your bestiary when a fight starts. Only the character you play learns this; other characters learn it when they are there themselves.

## What the table added to your canon

Facts the Narrator put on record also appear on the Canon page under **What the table added**. For each one you choose **Keep**, **Edit**, or **Strike** (the fact never happened, and neither players nor the Narrator read it again; **Undo** brings it back). A menu offers **Make it a truth**. The Narrator reads your canon as it plays, but never reads a secret's statement, and an invention it adds is checked against what you have written.
