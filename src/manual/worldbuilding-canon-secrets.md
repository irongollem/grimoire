This page covers two parts of the **Canon** page: **Secrets and questions** and **What is moving**. Both are yours alone. Players never see a clue, a force, a step or a clock, and they read a secret only once you reveal it.

## Secrets and clues

A secret is something the world is keeping back. Write it as one statement ("The duke poisoned his brother"), then give it clues the party can find.

1. Under **Secrets and questions**, type the statement into **A new secret, as one statement…** and press **Add secret**.
2. Select it in the list. Under **Clues**, press **Add a clue**.
3. Write the clue in a line ("A cracked vial in the duke's study") and say **Where it can be found**: **A place**, **A person**, **A beat** (a beat in a quest), or **Nowhere** if you have not decided yet. Press **Add clue**.

A secret wants three clues, so the party can miss two and still find it. The detail panel reminds you when a secret has fewer than three. **Place clues** can suggest clues for a secret; nothing is saved until you accept one.

### Where a clue shows up

A clue placed at a place, a person or a quest beat appears in a **Canon here** block on that place's page, on that NPC's sheet and in that beat's editor. Each clue there has the same **Found** tick, so you can tick it off right where it happened, without opening the Canon page.

### Found, and the secret's state

When the party finds a clue, tick **Found**. Unticking it takes the tick back. The list reads "1 of 3 clues found".

A secret's state is worked out from its clues. You never set it by hand:

| State | Mark | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hidden** | empty circle | No clue has been found. |
| **Partly known** | half circle | At least one clue is found. |
| **Revealed** | full circle | You have revealed it. |

Each state is a word and a shape, so it never depends on colour alone.

### Finding every clue does not reveal the secret

This is on purpose. A party can hold all three clues and still not have put them together. A secret becomes **Revealed** only when you press **Reveal to the party**. Grimoire asks you to confirm, then shows the secret on every player's screen. The panel says it plainly: "Finding every clue never reveals it by itself: revealing it to the players is your button."

Changed your mind? A revealed secret shows **Hide again**, which takes it off their screens.

Players read a secret only after you reveal it, and they never see its clues, found or not.

## Open questions

Beside the secrets is a plain list headed **Open questions**: things you keep open on purpose. Add one with **A new open question…** and **Add question**. Each is marked "Kept open: nobody settles this by invention". Nobody, the AI table included, settles an open question by making something up. Use it for the mysteries you have not decided yet, such as who the stranger at the inn really is. **Suggest questions** can propose a few; you accept or dismiss each one.

## What is moving

**What is moving** holds the forces at work in the world: a faction with an agenda, a cult, a coming war. Each force says who moves, what it wants in one line, what it does next, and optionally a clock that fills as it gets closer to its goal.

### Adding a force

Under **Who moves**, write a name or choose a faction, say what it wants in one line, and pick a **Clock size** (or **No clock**). Press **Add force**. **Suggest forces** proposes a force for each faction with an active agenda; you accept or dismiss each one, and nothing is saved before you accept.

### Steps

A force's steps are what it does next. Add them with **Add step** in the order you expect them. When one happens, tick **Reached**. The step furthest along reads **Now**, earlier reached steps read **Done**, and the rest read **Next**. You can move steps earlier or later, or remove them.

### The clock

The clock fills one tick at a time. Press **Tick forward** when the force makes progress and **Tick back** when the party sets it back. You can add a note ("Why, if you want to say…"). Ticking a step's **Reached** box also moves the clock one tick. Unticking it takes that tick back, so the clock never counts a step that has not happened.

The newest tick has an **Undo**.

If your campaign uses a calendar, each tick remembers the world's date as well as the evening it happened.

### The world moved

Beside the list is one log called **The world moved**: every force's ticks, newest first, each naming its force. Use it to remind yourself, before a session, what has been going on in the background.

### Where the party meets a force

On a force, **Link a quest** adds the quests where the party meets it. A quest can be linked once. Each link takes you to that quest.

## Tips

- Three clues for every secret, spread across places, people and beats, is a good habit.
- Place a clue on a quest beat and it turns up in the beat's editor, so you remember to give it out.
- Use **Hide again** if you revealed a secret too early.
