The Canon page (in the menu after Notes) holds the shape of your world. Every AI generator in Grimoire reads part of it, so what you write here steers what the tome makes for this campaign.

## The parts

- **Premise and tone.** The pitch, the themes, how play feels at the table, the voice and the look of the world, and the lines and veils your group agreed. Generators read all of it. Lines become "never include, in any form" and veils "only off the page". Players see what you share, never the voice or the look.
- **Truths.** Facts about the world, each marked **known to all**, **known to some** or **secret**. A truth known to all also reaches the generators.
- **What is moving.** Forces at work in the world, with their steps. Players never see them.
- **How the world works.** The calendar, today's date, the climate and the coins.
- **Secrets and open questions.** What you keep back, with the clues that point at it, and what you have not decided yet.
- **Lore.** Stories told in passages. You tell a passage to a character or to the whole party when they learn it.
- **Backstories.** One per character, written in passages by you and by the player.
- **The Chronicle.** Your session notes, in order: what happened.
- **What the table added.** Facts the AI table put on record while it played. Review them, and keep or strike each one.

## Connections: "about", "known to" and the others

An entry can be tied to people, factions, places, gods, items and other entries. The tie always reads from the entry outward.

- **About**: the entry tells of this person, place or thing; they are in the story. On the other side the tie reads **told of in**. A backstory that is *about* the old smuggler Wenna mentions her; it says nothing about whether Wenna knows it.
- **Known to**: this person, or this faction, knows what the entry says. On the other side it reads **knows**. Use it for who holds the knowledge, whether or not the entry is about them.
- **Took place at** (places only): where the entry happened. The other side reads **scene of**.
- **Led to** and **related to** (between entries): one event led to another, or two entries belong together.

So a character's past can be *about* their mentor and *known to* the rival who found out. Mark both when both are true.

**What "known to" changes for players.** A truth marked **known to some** reaches the journal of each character it is *known to*, and of each character in a faction it is *known to*. Nobody else sees it. For other entries, such as a backstory, who may read it is set with the entry's **Share** control, not by its ties. The ties tell the AI table who knows what, and who is in the story.

## Backstories

Open a character under Backstories to see their past. The player can write passages of their own (**Add to my story** in their journal), and you can edit every passage. Your own passages start untold: **Tell <character>** or **Tell the party** reveals them. **The people from this past** are the backstory's connections. **New NPC from this backstory** makes a hidden NPC tied *about* from the backstory, asks how they relate to the character, and opens them for you to fill in.

## The look

The Look field takes up to four pictures as well as words. **Describe from the pictures** writes a paragraph about how they are painted, for you to edit. Every picture the tome paints for this campaign follows the look, maps excepted.

## AI help

**Draft from a pitch** proposes a premise from your pitch, and **Suggest truths** proposes truths with their marks. Nothing is saved until you accept it. Discuss lines and veils with your players before you accept them, because accepting puts them into every AI generation at once.

## Bringing in a book

**Bring in a book** (at the top of the page) takes a campaign book apart into places, people, factions, gods, quests and canon in one pass.
